# Balkans Crime & Corruption Round-up — week ending 2026-11-14

## Key points
- **Serbia's post-election arrests of officials:** Inđija municipal president Marko Gašić and five others were arrested on 8 Nov over allegedly rigged road-marking tenders (about 27m dinars), former economy minister Rade Basta and seven others on 11 Nov over Beogradske elektrane donations (14.1m dinars), and Smederevo mayor Jasmina Vojinović (SNS) and two officials on 12 Nov over an illegal landfill. Detention was ordered for Zabela prison warden Rodoljub Rončević over alleged vote buying, and Niš prison warden Bratislav Dikić reportedly admitted he monitored SNS "capillary votes". Vučić and SNS call the Smederevo arrest political. All are suspicions only.
- **Open conflict inside Serbia's prosecution service:** a Supreme Public Prosecutor's Office commission says Belgrade chief prosecutor Nenad Stefanović set up a "para-prosecutorial system" and that some of his prosecutors may have abused office to gather information on the student movement. Its report has gone to the organised-crime prosecutor (TOK), and it has taken over 34 cases, including Savamala and Banjska. Stefanović filed a criminal complaint against commission chair Goran Ilić and a disciplinary complaint against Branko Stamenković.
- **Reported links between officials and criminal groups in Serbia (unproven):** former president Aleksandar Vučić and BIA official Marko Parezanović are to be called as witnesses in Darko Šarić's money-laundering trial, after Sky messages allegedly showed them negotiating his surrender. US-sanctioned Zvonko Veselinović, a former business partner of Milan Radoičić, flew from Sarajevo to Istanbul on 11 Nov. A former BIA inspector claims Radoičić left for Russia, but the Belgrade court says his travel ban stands. TOK froze the assets of Alta banka owner Davor Macura (about €380m allegedly taken out).
- **Police officers as suspects:** in Serbia, 15 officers (eight from Belgrade's criminal police on 8–9 Nov and seven from Bor on 13 Nov) have been arrested over the 2024 death in custody of Dalibor Dragijević. In Bosnia, Operation Vrtlog arrested 15 people and seized about 150 kg of drugs; those arrested include Darko Čajić, former police escort of ex-security minister Nenad Nešić. Istraga links the network to associates of Rusmir Isak.
- **Albania:** the Special Appeals Court convicted former police operations chief Oltion Bistri (Metamorfoza) of passive corruption and money laundering, raising his sentence to 7 years 4 months and confiscating €1.3m and five flats; he is reported to have fled towards Greece. SPAK sent the Çopja-Çela organisation to trial over more than 60 tonnes of cocaine, registered a complaint over resort permits that names PM Edi Rama and Belinda Balluku, and opened a wealth investigation into former SHISH head Vlora Hyseni.
- **Montenegro and Croatia, links between criminal groups and the justice system:** at his trial, former chief special prosecutor Milivoje Katnić accused the SDT of hiding SKY messages in which Kavač and Škaljari members allegedly planned his murder. A former Montenegrin police officer was arrested in Shkodër as an alleged member of the Krstović group. In Zagreb, Zdravko Mamić's former wife testified he took about €300,000 in cash to judge Zvonko Vekić.
