# Balkans brief — 3 November 2026

## Serbia transition watch — after the 25 October 2026 election

**[OCG/Corruption] Court orders 30 days' detention for police crime chiefs Kričak and Sekulović** — Overnight into 3 November the Belgrade Higher Court ordered up to 30 days' custody for Marko Kričak (head of the criminal police directorate) and Boško Sekulović (head of the crime-suppression service), arrested on 1 November. Each is charged with abuse of office; Kričak is also charged with ill-treatment and torture of the owner of restaurant "27" on 16 May. The court cites a risk they would influence witnesses. Event date: 3 November 2026 (ruling), arrests 1 November.

**[Political] Supreme Public Prosecutor's commission takes over cases of police abuse of students linked to Kričak** — Prosecutor Branko Stamenković said the commission supervising all 87 prosecution offices took over cases from the First Basic Prosecution Office in Belgrade, including the August 2025 allegations that students were mistreated in the Government garage and outside the Faculty of Law, in which Kričak is named. **[Political] Courts publish names of Vučić's pardons — Dijana Hrkalović and the driver who hit a student among them** — The Belgrade and Novi Sad higher courts released the names of those pardoned by Aleksandar Vučić before he resigned as president; the decisions are dated 27 September and reached the courts on 2 November. Belgrade's list: Dijana Hrkalović (trading in influence), Nebojša Đokić (money laundering), Marko Pavlović (attempted aggravated murder), Dragana Živadinović (abuse of office) and Miloš Dugalić (sentence); Novi Sad's list has 18 names. KoSSev counts 23 names in total. The student movement called the pardon of the driver who struck student Sonja Ponjavić "unacceptable — another dangerous precedent"; lawyer Vida Petrović Škero argues pardons issued without proper procedure are void. [Students] Event date: 2 November 2026.

**[OCG/Corruption] Alta banka owner Davor Macura detained over suspected €383m removed from the vault** — Three days after the central bank revoked Alta banka's licence, the organised-crime prosecution (TOK) detained owner Davor Macura (businessman close to SNS) and two bank treasury staff for 48 hours on suspicion of taking about 45 billion dinars (around €383 million) out of the bank's treasury.
