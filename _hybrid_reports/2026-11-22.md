# Balkans Hybrid Activity Report — week ending 2026-11-21

## Key points
- **Arrests in North Macedonia over an Iran-directed plot.** On 17 November ANB and the police said six Macedonian citizens from criminal circles prepared a terrorist attack in Greece on direct instructions from an Iranian national. Four were arrested in North Macedonia, one in Greece, and one is still at large. Israeli media report that Mossad assisted and that Israeli businessmen were the targets. On 20 November TV Telma said it would air more on the Iranian link on 22 November.
- **Sanctions and entry bans.** On 16 November the EU sanctioned Irish commentator Chay Bowes as a Russian propagandist. Albanian PM Edi Rama says Bowes targeted Albania's protests. Separately, Bosnia and Herzegovina has banned Milan Radoičić, Zvonko and Žarko Veselinović and at least five other Serbian citizens close to the former Vučić government from entering the country, declaring them national security threats. The decision took effect on 13 November and was reported on 19 November.
- **Kosovo countermeasure against Serbia.** On 16 November Kosovo banned all electricity trade with Serbian companies. The government says Serbia's grid operator EMS keeps cross-border interconnection lines blocked despite an Energy Community decision. Kurti warned that further escalation is possible.
- **Pattern: Milan Radoičić across three countries.** The suspected organiser of the 2023 Banjska attack appears in Serbia, where a Belgrade court issued a warrant for him on 17 November after he failed to report to police. He also appears in Bosnia and Herzegovina (the entry ban) and in Kosovo, where security expert Murat Makolli says, without evidence, that Russian intelligence helped him leave Serbia.
- **Montenegro opens a parliamentary track on Serbian-linked media influence.** On 16 November most members of the Security and Defence Committee backed a control hearing on possible hybrid foreign influence through national TV broadcasters, M:tel, Alta banka and Alta Pay Group. This follows PM Spajić's 2 November call for an inquiry. Nothing was logged for China this week.

## Serbia

- What is reported: BiH security agencies have banned Milan Radoičić, Zvonko and Žarko Veselinović and at least five other Serbian citizens close to the former Vučić government from entering the country, declaring them national security threats. The decision took effect on 13 November and was reported on 19 November. No public justification was given. Radoičić is accused of leading the 2023 Banjska paramilitary attack, and his whereabouts are unknown. Related crime logs add context. On 17 November Belgrade's Higher Court ordered his detention and issued a wanted notice after he failed to report to police on 15 November. Prosecutors allege he procured weapons, ammunition and explosives in Bosnia and stored them in Kosovo. The Veselinović brothers, both under US sanctions, flew from Sarajevo to Istanbul on 11 November (case RS-VESELINOVIC-GROUP). In October Kurti said Serbia uses Russian-style deniable methods against Kosovo and cited Radoičić (20261023-HY-XK-01).

**Kosovo–Serbia electricity interconnection dispute** — Countries: Kosovo. Methods: energy leverage. Status: claimed (16 Nov).
- What is reported: The Kosovo government says Serbia's transmission operator EMS has not unblocked the interconnection lines, despite an Energy Community Ministerial Council decision that took effect on 1 July. That is the government's claim. In response the government banned the purchase, sale, import and export of electricity with Serbian companies, whether direct or through transit and resale. Kurti called the ban a countermeasure and warned of further escalation. Critics in Pristina called the decision late and partial, and some said it would not cover the north.
