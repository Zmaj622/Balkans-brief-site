# Balkans Hybrid Activity Report — week ending 2026-10-24

## Key points
- **No official measures (expulsions, arrests, sanctions) were logged this week.** The one arrest mention is secondhand: the WSJ, as relayed by Klix.ba, reports that Serbian prosecutors have reportedly arrested seven suspects over the August Leipzig/Halle airport drone attack (a WSJ report, not a confirmed measure).
- **Claims dominate the week.** Four of the seven entries are claims: Albanian PM Edi Rama blames Russia and Iran for Albania's protests (two entries, 18–19 October, no evidence offered, Moscow denies); the WSJ says Putin ordered sabotage against NATO states with Serbia as a hub; Kosovo PM Albin Kurti says Serbia uses Russian-style deniable methods. Serbia's government rejected Kurti's claim and Russia's foreign ministry rejected Rama's.
- **New investigative finding on Serbia (18 and 21 October):** BIRN reports the Serbian foreign ministry's diaspora administration gave over €250,000 to diaspora groups that back SNS or promote right-wing positions, with recipients in North Macedonia and Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- **Pattern:** Russia is the alleged actor in Albania, Montenegro and, regionally, via Serbia, mostly through disinformation and proxy networks. Serbia appears both as an acting state (funding, proxy-group claims) and as a transit hub in the WSJ claim.

Organisations: PS (political party). State bodies acting: Government of Albania; Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs; State Police (Albania). Iran is also named as an acting state by Rama.

## Serbia

**Serbian foreign ministry funding of diaspora groups** — Countries: North Macedonia; Bosnia and Herzegovina; Serb diaspora. Methods: influence funding; political interference. Status: reported (BIRN investigation).
- What is reported: A BIRN investigation says the Serbian foreign ministry's diaspora administration allocated more than €250,000 to diaspora organisations that support the ruling SNS or promote right-wing positions. In North Macedonia, associations in Kumanovo such as Mir, sloga, stabilnost reportedly organised transport to SNS rallies. In Bosnia and Herzegovina, funded groups such as Baštionik (about €34,000, 2023–2025) took part in anti-LGBTQ+ campaigns and historical revisionism and promoted pro-Russian narratives. Findings are based on BIRN's analysis of the funding.
- Timeline:
  - 2026-10-18 — Vreme relays the BIRN investigation: over €250,000 to SNS-supporting diaspora groups, including associations in North Macedonia.
  - 2026-10-21 — Detektor (BIRN BiH), publishing the investigation on 20 October, adds the Bosnia and Herzegovina recipients (Baštionik, Jadovno 1941).
- People: none named. Organisations: SNS (political party); Mir, sloga, stabilnost (association); Baštionik (association); Jadovno 1941 (association). State bodies acting: Ministarstvo spoljnih poslova Srbije (ministry); Uprava za dijasporu (state body).
- Targets: North Macedonia; Bosnia and Herzegovina; Serb diaspora.
- Related crime cases: none.
- Sources: [Vreme](https://vreme.com/vesti/birn-ministarstvo-spoljnih-poslova-placa-grupe-iz-dijapsore-koje-podrzavaju-sns/) (Critical of ruling party (SNS)); [Detektor (BIRN BiH)](https://detektor.ba/2026/10/20/vise-od-250-000-evra-za-organizacije-koje-podrzavaju-sns-i-vucica-u-regionu/) (Western-donor funded).
