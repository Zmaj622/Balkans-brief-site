# Balkans Hybrid Activity Report — week ending 2026-10-17

## Key points
- **Official measure, follow-up (Montenegro vs Russia):** Rybar founder Mihail Zvinčuk, expelled from Montenegro after the 9 October detention near Kotor, was banned from entering Montenegro for one year, then returned to Moscow via Belgrade. He says Russia's foreign ministry and diplomatic network were involved. The EU says it is helping Montenegro resist hybrid threats and recalls that Zvinčuk has been under EU restrictive measures since June 2023.
- **Election-period claims (Serbia, vote on 25 October 2026):** two opposing claims, both unproven in the reporting. Ukraine's embassy in Belgrade says campaigners are spreading Russian propaganda. Aleksandar Vučić says Croatia, Pristina and Podgorica interfere in Serbia's election, and Belgrade sent Zagreb a protest note. Croatia's prime minister called this "spin".
- Russia is the acting state in four of the week's five entries, in Montenegro and Serbia. Only the Montenegro storyline involves an official measure.

## Russia

**Rybar network expulsion from Montenegro** — Countries: Montenegro. Methods: disinformation; espionage. Status: reported (EU statement, 14 October).
- What is reported: Mihail Zvinčuk, co-founder of the Rybar network, was expelled from Montenegro after the 9 October detention of five Russians (earlier entry, 10 October). The border police decision bans him from Montenegro for one year; Zvinčuk told Kotor police the group travelled from Banja Luka and was filming a documentary. After returning to Moscow via Belgrade, Zvinčuk said Russia's foreign ministry and diplomatic network were involved, and that the seized drone and cameras have not been returned. The EU says it supports Montenegro against hybrid threats.
- Timeline:
  - 2026-10-10 — Rybar publishes the border police decision banning Zvinčuk for one year, plus his statement to Kotor police. Dan and Aktuelno report it. Klix says he was in Bosnia and Herzegovina from 2 to 7 October.
  - 2026-10-12 — Zvinčuk is back in Russia. Zvinčuk says Russian state institutions were involved (a claim by Zvinčuk).
  - 2026-10-13 — EU spokesperson Christian Wigand tells RFE/RL the EU supports Montenegro against new and growing hybrid threats. Zvinčuk says Russian state institutions helped secure his release; Rybar says its team was in Montenegro legally (claims by Zvinčuk and Rybar).
- People: Mihail Zvinčuk (Rybar founder, expelled, entry ban); Valerija Zvinčuk (Rybar co-founder, expelled); Christian Wigand (EU spokesperson). Organisations: Rybar (Russian propaganda network). State bodies acting: Uprava policije (police); European Union (EU); Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (ministry).
- Targets: Montenegro.
- Related crime cases: ME-RYBAR-EXPULSION — Rybar network expulsion from Montenegro.
